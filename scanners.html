Game MiddlesScans DarkHorse Odds' Middles finder for game spread and total lines where two sportsbooks disagree enough that betting both sides creates a window where both bets can win (for example, one book at -3.5 and another at +4.5). It keeps only pairs with both legs priced -170 or better and covers spread, alternate spread, total, and alternate total markets. When a new or improved middle appears, Michael gets both legs with the exact book and price for each so he can place them himself.PCEvery 30 minutesMoved to PCdh_game_middles.py
Key-Number MiddlesA tighter version of the middles scan, built on Spanky's key-number craft from the Be Better Bettors podcast. Since Nov 10, 2026 it uses a data-based key set — 3, 7, 6, 4, 10, 14, 17 — the margins NFL games have landed on most since 2010 (Chet's scan of 4,499 games: 3 lands 14.6% of the time, 7 lands 8.7%, 6 lands 6.7%). It keeps only middles whose both-win margin set contains one of those numbers, because those are the margins games land on most often. Near-misses that don't touch a key number are thrown out, and clean pairs (both legs -140 or better) are flagged. The old tradition-based set (3, 7, 10, 14, 17, 21, 28) was retired Nov 10, 2026 — 21 and 28 land too rarely (2.5% and 1.5%) to keep, and 6 and 4 earned their way in. SCOPE FIX (Nov 11, 2026, Michael's ruling): football only — NFL and NCAAF. The scan had no league gate and spent Nov 10-11 banking NCAAB/NBA middles labeled with football keys; rows now qualify only when the identity's league token is NFL or NCAAF, fail-closed otherwise, and the basketball entries were purged from its state. Basketball middles live in the separate basketball cluster scan. MARGIN-LOGIC FIX (Nov 12, 2026, Michael's tap): the scan used to merge both-win margins and win+push endpoints into one "margins" list and treated any key in it as a both-win key — Kansas +5 / West Virginia -4 (Nov 11) and Jets +10 / Bills -8.5 (Nov 12) were delivered as both-win pairs when both are win+push only. The scan now splits them: strict both-win margins (integers strictly between the two lines) and win+push endpoints (whole-number lines), qualifies on either set containing a key, and labels each pair BOTH-WIN or WIN+PUSH honestly in the banked lines and state.PCEvery 30 minutesMoved to PCdh_key_number_middles.py
Basketball Key-Number MiddlesThe basketball sibling of the key-number scan, built Nov 10, 2026 off Chet's ESPN-history scan of 2,644 NBA games (2023-25) and 6,292 college games (2024-25). The honest finding: basketball has no football-style key numbers — scoring in 1s, 2s and 3s smooths margins into a plateau (the most common NBA margin, 7, lands just 5.7% of games vs football's 14.6% on 3) and totals into a smooth bell with no key totals at all. So it scores every NBA and college middle by its modeled both-win probability from the real landing rates and keeps spread middles at 10% or better and total middles at 8% or better. PLUS a Spanky tier (added the same night at Michael's tap): Spanky never named basketball key numbers either — his college baselines from Be Better Bettors are width rules at -110 (Ep 14: college totals need ~2.5 points for a +EV middle, 2 points is break-even; Ep 39: 1.5-point side middles are the baseline), so college rows also qualify, labeled SPANKY, at spread windows of 1.5+ points or total windows of 2.5+ points with both legs -125 or better. He gave no NBA width rule, so NBA rows stay on the modeled tier only. Both legs -170 or better overall, clean pairs (both -140 or better) flagged. Findings page: key-number-middles-nba-ncaab.html.PCEvery 30 minutesLive on PCdh_basketball_key_middles.py

SOG MiddlesA middles scan for NHL shots-on-goal props: it finds players whose shots line differs between books so an over at one book and an under at another can both win. Team-Total MiddlesThe key-20 team-total scan, un-gated Nov 10, 2026. DarkHorse provably lists no team totals anywhere (checked Oct 31, 2026: every finder's market tree, the browse table, and game pages), so the scan sat as a silent stub until Michael tapped a source hunt and Kalshi turned out to carry the market: its KXNFLTEAMTOTAL series posts a ladder of 'Team over X.5 points' binary markets for every NFL game (381 open markets across 14 games at first check). Team scores genuinely have key numbers — from Chet's count of 14,824 NFL team-games, a team lands on exactly 20 in 6.96% of games, 17 in 6.73%, 24 in 6.48% and 27 in 5.63% (the touchdown/field-goal lattice), versus 1-3.7% for ordinary scores. The scan pairs a Kalshi YES on a lower threshold with a NO on a higher one so both contracts pay when the team lands in the window between them; a pair qualifies only when the window contains 17, 20, 24 or 27, is at most 5 numbers wide around the team's market-implied median score, and the two asks combined cost less than the modeled landing probability justifies after a 2-cent fee buffer, with the worst case capped at -10 cents per $1 pair. First live read: 131 keyed candidate straddles, the best 1.6 cents short of +EV — the market is efficient tonight, so the scan correctly banked zero; it fires when a leg drifts. NFL only.PCEvery 30 minutesLive on PCdh_team_total_middles.py

Following Michael's rule, it only delivers pairs with profit strictly over 3%, and at least one leg must be placeable in New Jersey or on Kalshi, Polymarket US, or ProphetX. Each delivery shows both legs with book, price, and stake sizing.PCHourlyMoved to PCdh_arbitrage.py

Low HoldsFinds two-sided markets where the books' combined hold is 1% or less — meaning the pair of prices is so efficient that hedging both sides costs almost nothing, and occasionally pays on both outcomes. A small positive hold is a near-zero-cost hedge, not guaranteed profit, and the scan says so. New or changed pairs are delivered with both legs' books and prices.PCHourlyMoved to PCdh_low_holds.py

Steam-Side Stale-Book ScanWhen a line steams — moves sharply across the market because of news or heavy action — some books are slow to follow. This scan compares every New Jersey book against the market median right after a move and flags the books still hanging the old, now-stale number. Those stale prices are the value: Michael can bet the pre-move number everyone else has already abandoned.PCEvery 30 minutesMoved to PCsteam_side.py
Reverse Line Movement (RLM)Watches for lines moving against the betting public — for example, a team getting most of the bets while its line gets worse — a Spanky-style signal from Be Better Bettors that sharp money is on the other side. When the split between bet percentage and line direction is big enough, the game is flagged with both numbers shown. Flags are information, not automatic plays.PCHourlyMoved to PCrlm.py
Leader-FollowerTracks which sportsbooks consistently move their lines first and which ones copy later. When a 'leader' book moves and a 'follower' book hasn't caught up yet, the follower's stale line gets flagged — the same stale-price idea as the steam-side scan, driven by book behavior instead of news. It also logs the data that shows which books deserve the leader label.PCHourlyMoved to PCleader_follower.py
Hung NumbersAnother Spanky pattern from Be Better Bettors: a 'hung' number is a line a book refuses to move even while the rest of the market moves around it. This scan flags books holding a number the market has left behind, because that stubborn price is often the best one available. Flags come with the book, its line, and where the market consensus sits.PCHourlyMoved to PChung_number.py

NCAAF First-Score & Lead-Change ScanThe college football version of the first-score scan: for every FBS game this season, which team scored first, how it scored (field goal, touchdown, or safety), how fast, and how many times the lead changed hands. Michael uses the patterns for in-play middling, and the tally also tracks how often the team that scores first goes on to win — in the season backfill (687 games through Nov 10) the first-scoring team won 70.9% of the time, the strongest first-score edge of any league tracked, with the home team scoring first 59.7% of the time and a median first score of 5:35.
