Close to the Number — What's Working, What's Not, and Why

Season-to-date closeness stats from the Football Closing Lines workbook, through Nov 4, 2026 (props through Nov 1). Every morning's 6:44 AM run refreshes the Combined Summary tab in the workbook; this page is the plain-English read of what those numbers mean.

✅ What's working

NHL 1-goal tracking — the sharpest signal on the board

Favorites have won by exactly 1 goal in 25.5% of games (42 of 165) — better than one game in four. No other sport comes close to landing on a single number that often.

Why it works: hockey is low-scoring and the puck line is almost always −1.5, so a one-goal favorite win is a routine outcome, not a fluke. That's also why the 5-goal window is useless here — 92.7% of puck lines and 97.0% of totals land within 5, which says nothing. The brief headlines NHL at within-1-goal for exactly this reason. Average distances are tiny (2.12 on the spread, 1.81 on the total): the books price hockey tight, and the 1-goal split is where the information lives.

NFL rushing props — the most predictable prop market

18.4% of rushing lines land within 5 yards (88 of 479 players), with the smallest average miss of any prop type at 20.8 yards.

Why it works: rushing volume is the most stable thing in football — a lead back's carries are scripted by the game plan and hold up week to week, so the closing number sits closer to the outcome than passing or receiving lines do.

MLB run lines — tight, when there's a season

Average miss of just 2.56 runs on the run line and 2.57 on totals across the tracked games, with the favorite winning by exactly 1 in 12.5% of them.

Why it works: baseball scoring is low-variance per game and the run line is a fixed −1.5, so results cluster near the number. Caveat: the tracked sample is small (16 games) and the season ended Oct 28 — this section wakes back up in the spring.

⚠️ Mixed

Football spreads — the honest middle

29.6% of spreads land within 5 points (207 of 699 games), average miss 10.75 points. Totals run a little worse: 26.2% within 5, average miss 11.80.

Why: the closing spread is an efficient market price — most games aren't close to it, and the ones that are cluster around the key numbers (3 and 7). Totals miss wider because scoring variance compounds across both teams: one shootout quarter blows up a number a spread can survive.

NFL receiving props — better than passing, still swingy

15.9% land within 5 yards (176 of 1,107 players), average miss 22.9 yards.

Why: target share is game-plan and coverage dependent — a receiver can be schemed away for a week — but the sheer number of tracked receivers means the misses average out more than the QB market's do.

❌ What's not working

NFL passing props — the least predictable number tracked

Only 6.8% of QB passing lines land within 5 yards (16 of 237), and the average miss is a huge 57.7 yards — nearly three times the rushing miss.

Why it doesn't work: passing yardage is the most game-script-sensitive stat in the sport. Blowouts bench starters early, trailing teams throw 50 times, one 60-yard bomb moves a total by itself, and a mid-game QB change zeroes the line out entirely. The closing number is a guess about the whole shape of the game, not just the player — so it lands far from the result far more often.

"Favorite by exactly 1" in football — a unicorn

It's happened 16 times in 699 games (2.3%).

Why it doesn't work: football scoring comes in 3s and 7s, so final margins land on key numbers and skip 1 almost entirely — the mirror image of hockey, where 1 is the single most common favorite margin. Any angle built on football games landing on exactly 1 is fighting the scoring system itself.
