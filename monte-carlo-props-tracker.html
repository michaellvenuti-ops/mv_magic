How it works (Mack, Vol. 2 — player-prop Monte Carlo sims, in Python). A SECOND OPINION on props, not a copy of the Sixers Normal model. For each player and each of points / rebounds / assists (plus PRA where posted), the sim draws 10,000 versions of tonight: (1) Minutes ~ Normal(last-10 average minutes, last-10 minutes SD), clipped to [0, 48]. (2) Per-minute rate ~ Normal(last-10 per-minute rate, last-10 per-minute-rate SD) — minutes and rate vary independently, so the sim has fatter tails than a closed-form Normal. (3) Stat = rate x minutes x opponent factors (points scale by opponent DRtg / league DRtg; every stat scales by opponent pace / league pace; points get a +1% home bump). (4) P(Over) = share of the 10,000 sims above the line. PRA is simulated from its own per-game series, not by summing three independent draws. A prop flags when the sim edge over the book's implied probability is ≥ 5%, and the flagged side also hit in at least 4 of the player's last 10 games. Paper only — $100 flat per flag at the posted price, graded the next morning. Lines: HaterPicks public game pages (best Over/Under price at tracked US books, recorded at scan time). DarkHorse snapshot becomes the primary source when the pull is live. A player listed OUT is skipped; a questionable player is flagged with a QUESTIONABLE tag and a DNP grades as a void. Stability note: the sim is deliberately simpler than a full hierarchical model — no regression of rate on usage. A per-player OLS regression on 13–17 games was rejected in the Sixers build (it projected Embiid at 15.8 vs a 20.0 last-10 mean); the same rejection applies here — the last-10 rate is the flagger.
