The 9-8-7 Principle

This trick uses the properties of the out-faro shuffle. After 3 out-faros, the deck is interleaved such that specific values (the 9s, 8s, and 7s) end up in predictable positions in the indicator pile.

By splitting the deck and forcing a card, you create a system where the "indicator" card you land on tells you exactly how many cards to count in the target pile to reach the spectator's chosen card.

The Math Behind the Berglas Effect
The Berglas Effect is considered by many to be the holy grail of card magic. A spectator freely names any card and any number — and that card is found at that exact position. With a memorized deck, this becomes mathematically solvable in seconds.
THE CORE FORMULA
Cut Size = (Current Position − Target Position + 52) mod 52
Current Position — where the named card sits right now in your memorized stack (1–52)
Target Position — the number the spectator named
+52 mod 52 — handles wrap-around when the card is already past the target
Result — how many cards to cut from top to bottom
WORKED EXAMPLE
Run the solver above to see a live worked example here.
HOW A PERFORMER USES THIS
Step 1 — Know your stack. With a memorized deck you instantly know the position of any named card. When the spectator says "Seven of Hearts" you mentally locate it — say position 23.

Step 2 — Hear the number. The spectator names a position — say 10.

Step 3 — Calculate the cut. (23 − 10 + 52) mod 52 = 13. You need to cut 13 cards.

Step 4 — Execute invisibly. Under the cover of a false shuffle, a table cut, or simply squaring the deck you move exactly 13 cards from top to bottom. The Seven of Hearts is now at position 10.

Step 5 — Reveal. Count down to the named position and reveal the named card. To the audience it appears impossible.
WHY THE FORMULA WORKS
Think of the deck as a circular loop of 52 positions. When you cut N cards from the top to the bottom every card shifts up by N positions. So a card at position 23 moves to position 23 − N. Setting that equal to the target and solving for N gives you the cut size. The mod 52 wraps around so if the cut would be negative (card already above target) you go the long way around the circle instead.

Gilbreath & 8-Kings Theory

The cards in this trick are set up to exploit the Gilbreath principle. You could start with the Si Stebbins stack, in which the suits appear in cyclic fashion every 4 cards (CHSD) and the values also appear in cyclic fashion every 13 cards.

Any arrangement of the 13 values is acceptable. In fact, this stack—called the 8-Kings stack—has the advantage that the cards do not form a mathematical pattern: 8 K 3 10 2 7 9 5 Q 4 A 6 J. It can be memorized by this rhyming mnemonic: "8 Kings ThreaTen To Save 95 Ladies For One Sick Knave!".

The Strategy: After the cards have been cut, dealt, and riffle shuffled, you deal 14 cards (7 each). You know the first 4 cards will contain one of each suit, so as soon as you glance at your facedown cards, you know exactly what the other 2 suits are. Because of the Gilbreath principle, all card values will be represented among the first 13 cards; safely ignore the 14th card (the last faceup card) as it will simply duplicate a value already on the table.

Advanced Note (2 Predictions): This trick uses 2 predictions—a double application of the Gilbreath principle. Inspired by the "Schizoid Rosary" invented by Max Maven, this method guarantees that specific distributions (like red/black or picture cards) remain predictable even after shuffles like the "smoosh" or "rosary" shuffle.

The "Heart Lover" Rules & Parity Principle

The 3 Pairing Rules:

  • No Hearts: Turn cards face to face (nothing to see, no matter how flipped).
  • Both Hearts: Turn cards back to back (see a heart no matter how flipped).
  • 1 Heart: Arrange so the heart is faceup.

The Math & Checkerboard Deal: Numbering positions 1 to 20 places hearts faceup in odd positions and facedown in even positions. Dealing into 4 rows of 5 on a checkerboard maps odd positions to light squares and even positions to dark squares. Consolidating into target square B2 ensures all hearts finish faceup while non-hearts finish facedown!

♠ 21-Card Princess Lab
Click Reset to deal 21 random cards. Think of any card and click the column it is in.

Why the 11th Position?

After the first deal, the column containing the selected card is picked up second, which places the card in positions 8–14. During the second deal, it lands in the third, fourth, or fifth position in the column, placing it in the 10th–12th position overall. Finally, after the third deal, the card moves to the fourth position of its column, which, when gathered, forces it into the 11th position of the deck.

Performance Strategy

The magician begins by showing 21 cards, displayed in 3 columns of cards, each with 7 cards. The volunteer is asked to think of a card and to indicate which column it is in. The cards are gathered, one column at a time, with the selected pile sandwiched between the other 2 columns. The 21 cards are redealt into 3 columns, one card at a time, with the first card going to column 1, the second card going to column 2, and so on, and the spectator is again asked which column the card is in. Again, the columns are gathered with the selected column between the other 2. This process is repeated one more time—after which the magician deals all the cards into a single pile, and after a bunch of cards are dealt, he or she claims that the next card that is turned over will be the selected card. Although the volunteer is often skeptical, the magician turns over the selected card.

The Reveal: Deal about 15 cards faceup, noting the 11th card. Ask, “Would you be surprised if the next card I turned over was your card?” They will be skeptical. Instead of dealing the 16th card, turn the 11th card face-down!

Aronson Spelling Demo
Theory — Why This Cut Works (ACAAN Math):
In a memorized stack, every card has a known, fixed position P (1–52, counting from the top). This is a spelling variant of ACAAN (Any Card at Any Number): instead of a spectator naming a number, the "number" N is fixed by the letter-count of the freely named card's own full name (e.g. "TEN OF HEARTS" spelled with no spaces — T-E-N-O-F-H-E-A-R-T-S — is 11 letters).

Cutting C cards from the top to the bottom rotates the whole stack: every card's position decreases by C, wrapping around (mod 52) since a cut is circular. So a card starting at position P ends up at position P − C. We want that to land on N (its own letter-count), so:
C = P − N  (add 52 if negative)
Once cut, dealing one card per letter always ends on the named card as the very last card dealt — because it's sitting exactly N cards down.

Worked example (from this stack): the 10♥ sits at position 10. "TEN OF HEARTS" has 11 letters. Since 10 − 11 = −1, add 52 to get C = 51.

Practical tip: cutting 51 cards off the top sounds unwieldy, but it's mathematically identical to moving just the complementary 52 − 51 = 1 card from the bottom to the top instead — same final order, far less handling. In general, whenever C is more than half the deck, cut 52 − C cards from the bottom to the top instead; it produces the exact same arrangement with a smaller, faster action.

WHY MEMORIZE A DECK?
The Key Card — In the beginning there was the key card: a known card lying next to a selected card. Cutting without shuffling, or dealing cards one at a time, the card following the key card is the selection.

Then came the idea of making every card in the deck a key card all in a known order. If a card is taken we learn its identity by looking at the one that was above it. Thus we arrive at the concept of a memorized deck.

For a deeper history of stacks and memorized decks visit deceptionary.com/aboutstacks

2P–6P Hold'emDeals 2-card hole hands for 2 through 6 players plus 5 community cards with burn cards in between. Dealer receives the last hand.
Bottom Deal (Dealer)When checked the dealer's final card is forced from position 52 (bottom of deck) instead of dealing normally. Used to demonstrate bottom dealing in a controlled environment.
Clear TableHides the poker arena and clears all dealt hands from the display.
DEMOS
Jonah Poker — Demo 1Loads a 10-card bank from the Particle stack with the 9♣ on top. User is dealt first — the 9♣ (the Jonah card) goes straight to the User every time. The remaining 9 cards randomize each load so the hand looks different but the Jonah always lands in the same spot.
Jonah Poker — Demo 2Same 10-card bank reversed so 9♣ is on the bottom. Dealer is dealt first for 9 cards then the 10th card is bottom-dealt to the Dealer — the Dealer is doomed every time.
Gilbreth Shuffle DemoDemonstrates the key property of the Gilbreth Shuffle: two consecutive Gilbreth Shuffles always restore the deck to its original order regardless of starting position.
NDO → Mnemonica NDOTransforms the standard US NDO into the specific Mnemonica NDO starting order used for the faro entry procedure.
Mnemonica NDO → MnemonicaStep-by-step faro entry procedure to reach Mnemonica from its specific new deck order. Execute each step in sequence — the deck arrives at full Mnemonica order in a precise number of faro shuffles.
Mnemonica 8-Faro CycleDemonstrates how Mnemonica cycles through related stack states under repeated Out-Faros returning to full Mnemonica after exactly 8 shuffles.
Aronson Stay-StackLoads the Aronson stack performs one Out-Faro then uses Verify Stack to confirm the deck is still Aronson (cut-equivalent). Demonstrates the Stay-Stack property live.
Aronson Spelling Demo (ACAAN)Select any card from the loaded Aronson stack and click Explain & Cut. Calculates and performs the exact single cut so that spelling the card's full name (e.g. "TEN OF HEARTS") lands it as the very last card dealt. Includes a persistent theory panel explaining the underlying math (cut = stack position − letter count, mod 52) with a worked example and a practical tip for executing large cuts efficiently.
NDO → Particle / Particle NDO → Stack / Stack → NDOStep-by-step entry and exit procedures for the Particle stack. Each demo shows the precise faro and cut sequence needed to move between Particle and NDO in both directions.
Stack CompareSelect any two known stacks from the dropdowns and compare them side by side in a 52-row table. Color coding: green = exact match, gold = swap pair, blue = same value different suit, red = completely different.
Faro Cycles DemoInteractive faro cycle analysis. Shows how many Out-Faros it takes for any card or group of cards to return to their starting positions. Useful for understanding the mathematical periodicity of the faro shuffle.
NINE CARD SPELLER SUITE (JIM STEINMEYER)
Nine Card SpellerLoads a fixed 9-card setup: random fillers at positions 1,2,5,6,8,9, the 2♥ at position 3, an ambigram card at position 4, and the Q♠ (art depicting her holding the 2♥) at position 7. Spelling TWO, then O-F, then HEARTS, then M-A-G-I-C — dealing one card per letter into a table pile and dropping the remainder on top each time — always delivers the 2♥ on the final letter. The other two cards sit quietly in place, already staged for the two demos below.
Queen's PrisonerSame fixed setup, 2 rounds. Round 1 spells TWO-OF-HEARTS-MAGIC and reveals the 2♥, exactly as above. Simply gathering the cards afterward — no cut needed — delivers the Q♠ to position 3 automatically. Spelling the identical phrase again lands on her: the Queen, holding your card.
Is It Real... Or Is It Magic?Same fixed setup, 3 rounds. Spelling TWO-OF-HEARTS-MAGIC three times in a row — gathering normally between each round — reveals the 2♥, then the Q♠, then the ambigram card, in that exact order, with zero cuts required at any point. The final reveal is a clickable 3D flip card you can rotate between "MAGIC!" and "IS IT REAL?"
Truth or LieThree piles of three. Pick a pile and remember its bottom card (kept secret from the magician). Gathering with your chosen pile on top plants your card 3rd from the top automatically. Now lie — name any other card entirely. The magician spells the lie's value, then O-F, then the lie's suit, then T-R-U-T-H instead of magic — and your real card appears anyway, because the letter-counting math only depends on word length, never on which card is actually named.
