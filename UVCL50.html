Virtual Card Lab v10 — User Guide

DISPLAY
🃏 Peek ModeFlips all cards face down showing only their position number. Click any card to reveal it for 1 second then it hides again. Use this to drill stack memorization — guess each card before you click to reveal. Toggle off to return to normal view.
⇧ Upjog ModeToggle Upjog Mode ON then click any cards to upjog them (they rise with an orange glow). Once your selection is complete use Upjogged to Top or Upjogged to Bottom to strip the upjogged group to the top or bottom of the deck in their original relative order. Toggle off to cancel. Forms the basis of the Chinese Shuffle system.
▦ FullscreenExpands the card display area to fill the entire screen hiding the sidebar. Click again or press Escape to return to normal view. Especially useful on iPhone and iPad where screen space is limited.
Size: Small / Medium / LargeChanges the display size of all 52 cards. On desktop Small and Medium shrink the grid to 52% and 76% of the available width so all 13 columns remain but cards are physically smaller. Large fills the full width. On iPad and iPhone sizes use fluid auto-fill columns to fit the screen.
Card SwapClick any card to select it (highlighted in gold) then click a second card to swap their positions. A quick way to manually adjust the stack order one swap at a time.
Multi-Select (Ctrl+Click)Hold Ctrl (or Cmd on Mac) and click multiple adjacent cards to select a group. Selected cards highlight in gold. Use with Move Selected Left or Move Selected Right to shift the group one position at a time. Use Clear Selection to deselect all.
Move Selected Left / RightShifts a group of Ctrl+Click selected cards one position left or right as a unit. Cards must be contiguous (adjacent) — a warning appears in the log if they are not. The selection follows the cards so you can keep pressing to continue shifting.
MECHANICS & SHUFFLES
Faro OUTPerfect 1-for-1 weave — top card stays at position 1, bottom card stays at position 52. 8 consecutive Out-Faros returns any 52-card deck to its original order. Completely deterministic — same result every time.
Faro INPerfect 1-for-1 weave — top card moves to position 2. Used in stack entry procedures and the Faro Navigator to precisely position any card. 52 consecutive In-Faros restore the deck.
Reverse ShuffleReverses the entire 52-card deck end to end. Position 1 becomes 52, position 2 becomes 51, and so on.
Gilbreth ShuffleReverses the top 26 cards then interleaves them with the bottom 26. Key property: two consecutive Gilbreth Shuffles always restore the deck to its original order regardless of starting position.
Cut Deck (26)Moves the top 26 cards to the bottom — a standard half-deck cut.
RandomizeFisher-Yates full randomization of all 52 cards. Used as the starting point for Chinese Shuffle Lab demos.
Shift Left / RightShift Left moves the top card to the bottom (one-card cycle left). Shift Right moves the bottom card to the top (one-card cycle right).
Reverse Top 26Reverses only the top 26 cards. Bottom 26 untouched. Used in the Spanish NDO → Mnemonica entry procedure (Step 2).
Reverse Bottom 26Reverses only the bottom 26 cards. Top 26 untouched.
Unequal Faro Top 18Cuts the top 18 cards and out-faros them into the top of the remaining 34. Used in the Spanish NDO → Mnemonica procedure (Step 3).
Cut to Selected Card to Top / FaceClick any card to select it then use these buttons to cut the deck so that card becomes position 1 (top) or position 52 (face/bottom).
Overhand ShuffleSimulates a real overhand shuffle — runs random-sized packets (1–6 cards) from the top into the other hand, building a new pile. Fully randomizing with no anchored cards. Each press produces a different result.
Riffle ShuffleSimulates a real-world riffle shuffle. Splits the deck at a random point near the middle (positions 20–32), then interleaves both halves imperfectly with a weighted drop probability (65% single card, decreasing for 2 or 3). No anchored cards — positions 1 and 52 will both change. Seven consecutive riffle shuffles produce a statistically random deck.
Tabled RiffleA controlled riffle shuffle performed on the table. Splits at a random point (positions 22–30), anchors the top card of the right packet at position 1 and the bottom card of the left packet at position 52, then imperfectly interleaves the middle. Different from Faro Out because the split is random and the interleave is uneven.
Running CutDivides the deck into 6 equal packets and cycles them so each packet moves to where the previous one was. A running cut looks casual and natural but is a controlled operation — use it to reposition cards by a predictable number of positions.
Milk to Top / Milk to BottomStrips the top card and bottom card simultaneously and places them together. Milk to Top places both on top of the remaining deck (positions 1 and 2). Milk to Bottom places both at the bottom (positions 51 and 52). Fundamental move in Chinese Shuffle work and packet handling.
Upjogged to Top / BottomAfter selecting cards with Upjog Mode, these buttons strip the entire upjogged group to the top or bottom of the deck in their original relative order.
Cut at N 🧪Enter any number 1–51 and click 🧪 CUT to move exactly that many cards from top to bottom. Useful for precise positional cuts.
FALSE WORK (STACK PROTECTION)
Zarrow ShuffleSwaps the top 2 cards with the bottom 2. Cards in positions 3–50 remain completely undisturbed. Appears to be a full riffle shuffle while preserving the stack completely.
Blind Faro (Top 2 Center)Moves the top 2 cards to positions 25–26 (center of the deck). Appears to lose cards in a shuffle while keeping them locatable by position math.
Push-Through False ShuffleSimulates a riffle shuffle where the two halves are pushed through each other and squared up. The top 2 and bottom 2 cards swap positions while the middle 48 remain completely undisturbed. Appears more convincing than the Zarrow in performance.
Running CutWhen used as false work, a running cut can appear to mix the deck casually while actually cycling the stack by a predictable number of positions. Combine with a known cut-equivalent stack for invisible stack maintenance.
About False WorkFalse shuffles and false cuts leave the deck in the same order while appearing to mix the cards. Because the deck order does not change the Virtual Card Lab shows no movement — this is intentional and correct. Use the Session Log to confirm the operation was recorded. False work demos in UVCL focus on entry and exit points: what position key cards are in before and after, confirming the stack is preserved.

Dial SequenceEnter a sequence using digits for Run (e.g. 3 = run 3 cards), parentheses for Toss (e.g. (2) = toss 2), and braces for Milk (e.g. {4} = milk 4). Use dashes as separators. Example: 2124211 is the classic Mnemonica dialing sequence.

WHY MEMORIZE A DECK?
The Key Card — In the beginning there was the key card: a known card lying next to a selected card. Cutting without shuffling, or dealing cards one at a time, the card following the key card is the selection.

Then came the idea of making every card in the deck a key card all in a known order. If a card is taken we learn its identity by looking at the one that was above it. Thus we arrive at the concept of a memorized deck.

For a deeper history of stacks and memorized decks visit deceptionary.com/aboutstacks
NDO STACK PRESETS
New Deck Order (NDO)US Bicycle factory order: A→K Hearts, A→K Clubs, K→A Diamonds, K→A Spades. Essential baseline for faro math and stack entry procedures.
Spanish NDOA→K in all four suits CHaSeD order. The starting point for the Mnemonica faro entry procedure — reach Mnemonica in 3 steps from here.
French NDOK→A in all four suits. Common in Tally-Ho and European Bicycle decks out of the box.
Particle NDOThe specific new deck order used as the starting point for Particle stack entry procedures.
Mnemonica NDOThe specific new deck order Tamariz uses when entering Mnemonica via the faro procedure.
MEMORIZED DECK STACKS
MnemonicaJuan Tamariz's world-famous memorized stack. Exceptionally rich in built-in effects — poker hands, spelling tricks, Si Stebbins-style math, and suit separation routines. The gold standard for serious memdeck work.
ParticleJoshua Jay's memorized stack. A Stay Stack with top/bottom mirror symmetry — the top 26 and bottom 26 mirror each other in value and color enabling a unique set of visual and mathematical effects.
My CHaSeDPersonal CHaSeD stack grouped in sets of 4 by suit. Returns to its original order in exactly 2 Out-Faros — useful for fast resets in performance.
MemorandumWoody Aragon's memorized stack. Designed with strong poker and gambling demonstrations while maintaining full memdeck capabilities.
AronsonSimon Aronson's memorized stack. Famous for built-in poker deals, spelling effects, and the Stay Stack property. A favorite for ACAAN-style effects.
OsterlindRichard Osterlind's Breakthrough Card System. A mathematical cyclical stack preferred by mentalists — the position of any card is instantly calculable using a simple formula with no memorization required.
NikolaLouis Nikola's classic memorized stack — one of the earliest published memdeck systems. Historically important and still fully functional for standard memdeck work.
Eight KingsMnemonic cyclical stack using the phrase “Eight Kings Threatened To Save Nine Fair Ladies For One.” Suits rotate CHaSeD. Easy to memorize and reconstruct from scratch — great entry-level stack.
Boris WildFormula-based memdeck — arguably the easiest to learn. Values increment by 1 per group of 4 suits rotating CHaSeD. Ideal for performers who want reliable position math without a full memorization commitment.
JoyalThe Joyal memorized stack. Strong poker hand demonstrations and a balanced suit distribution supporting both memdeck and gambling-style routines.
Tetra RedA color-separated stack where all red cards occupy specific positions and all black cards occupy the rest. Primary target stack for the Chinese Shuffle Lab — Random → Tetra Red demo.
RedfordThe Redford memorized stack with its own unique built-in properties and poker deal capabilities.
MATHEMATICAL STACKS
Classic Stebbins (+3)Values increase by 3 with each card suits rotating CHaSeD. The oldest published mathematical stack. Instantly angle-vulnerable (the pattern is visible to observers) but excellent for learning stack math concepts.
My StebbinsPersonal Stebbins variant starting at a different value offset. Retains all formula benefits with a slightly less predictable opening sequence.
Stebbins +4 / +5Si Stebbins variants with value increments of 4 and 5. Denser mathematical patterns less visually predictable than +3 same instant position-calculation benefit.
Stebbins +2Si Stebbins variant with a value increment of 2. Produces a very rapid cycling pattern — mathematically equivalent to +11 going in reverse. Values cycle A,3,5,7,9,J,K,2,4,6,8,10,Q repeating with suits rotating CHaSeD.
Red Stebbins 9C to FaceA Stebbins variant cut so the 9 of Clubs sits at the face (position 52) of the deck. Used as a specific starting point for certain Stebbins-based routines.
Latin FormulaAncient cyclic stack (Rosary/Chapelet) based on a Latin mnemonic phrase encoding the sequence A,5,9,J,6,4,2,K,7,8,Q,3,10 in CHaSeD suit order. First described by Guyot in 1769 — one of the oldest published stack systems in history.
