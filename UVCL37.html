Virtual Card Lab v10 — User Guide

DISPLAY
🃏 Peek ModeFlips all cards face down and shows their position number. Click any card to reveal it for 1 second, then it hides again. Use this to drill stack memorization — guess each card before you click to reveal. Toggle off to return to normal view.
Size: Small / Medium / LargeChanges the display size of all cards. Small fits the full 52-card grid at 13 per row. Medium and Large scale up for easier reading.
Card SwapIn normal view, click any card to select it (highlighted in gold), then click a second card to swap their positions. A quick way to manually adjust the stack order.
MECHANICS & SHUFFLES
Faro OUTPerfect weave — top card stays at position 1. 8 consecutive Out-Faros returns any deck to its original order. Used to cycle a memorized stack back to itself.
Faro INPerfect weave — top card moves to position 2. Used in stack entry procedures and the binary Faro Navigator to precisely position any card.
Reverse ShuffleReverses the entire 52-card deck end to end.
Gilbreth ShuffleReverses the top 26 cards then randomly interleaves them with the bottom 26.
Cut Deck (26)Moves the top 26 cards to the bottom — a standard half-deck cut.
RandomizeFisher-Yates full randomization of all 52 cards.
Shift LeftMoves the top card to the bottom (one-card cycle left).
Shift RightMoves the bottom card to the top (one-card cycle right).
Reverse Top 26Reverses only the top 26 cards. Bottom 26 untouched. Used in the Spanish NDO → Mnemonica entry procedure (Step 2).
Reverse Bottom 26Reverses only the bottom 26 cards. Top 26 untouched.
Unequal Faro Top 18Cuts the top 18 cards and out-faros them into the top of the remaining 34. Used in the Spanish NDO → Mnemonica procedure (Step 3).
Cut at N 🧪Enter any number 1–51 and click 🧪 CUT to move exactly that many cards from top to bottom.
FALSE WORK (STACK PROTECTION)
Zarrow ShuffleSwaps the top 2 cards with the bottom 2. Cards in positions 3–50 remain completely undisturbed. Simulates a riffle shuffle while preserving the stack.
Blind FaroMoves the top 2 cards to positions 25–26 (center of the deck). Appears to lose cards in a shuffle while keeping them locatable.
WHY MEMORIZE A DECK?
The Key Card — In the beginning there was the key card: a known card lying next to a selected card. Cutting without shuffling, or dealing cards one at a time, the card following the key card is the selection.

Then came the idea of making every card in the deck a key card, all in a known order. If a card is taken, we learn its identity by looking at the one that was above it. Thus we arrive at the concept of a memorized deck.

For a deeper history of stacks and memorized decks, visit deceptionary.com/aboutstacks
STACK PRESETS
New Deck Order (NDO)US Bicycle factory order: A→K Hearts, A→K Clubs, K→A Diamonds, K→A Spades. Essential baseline for faro math and stack entry procedures.
Spanish NDOA→K in all four suits, CHaSeD order. The starting point for the Mnemonica faro entry procedure — reach Mnemonica in 3 steps from here.
French NDOK→A in all four suits. Common in Tally-Ho and European Bicycle decks. Useful when working with European cards out of the box.
Particle NDOCustom new deck order used as the starting point for Particle stack entry procedures.
MnemonicaJuan Tamariz's world-famous memorized stack. Exceptionally rich in built-in effects — poker hands, spelling tricks, Si Stebbins-style math, and suit separation routines. The gold standard for serious memdeck work.
AronsonSimon Aronson's memorized stack. Famous for its built-in poker deals, spelling effects, and the Stay Stack property. A favorite among card workers who focus on ACAAN-style effects.
NikolaLouis Nikola's classic memorized stack — one of the earliest published memdeck systems. Historically important and still fully functional for standard memdeck work.
Eight KingsMnemonic cyclical stack using the phrase “Eight Kings Threatened To Save Nine Fair Ladies For One.” Suits rotate CHaSeD. Easy to memorize and reconstruct from memory on the fly. Great entry-level stack.
Boris WildFormula-based memdeck — arguably the easiest to learn. Values increment by 1 per group of 4, suits rotate CHaSeD. Ideal for performers who want reliable position math without a full memorization commitment.
MemorandumWoody Aragon's memorized stack. Designed with a strong focus on natural-feeling poker and gambling demonstrations while maintaining full memdeck capabilities.
OsterlindRichard Osterlind's Breakthrough Card System. A mathematical cyclical stack preferred by mentalists — position of any card can be calculated instantly using a simple formula, no memorization required.
Classic StebbinsValues increase by 3 with each card, suits rotate CHaSeD. The oldest published mathematical stack. Instantly angle-vulnerable (pattern is visible to spectators) but excellent for learning stack math concepts.
My StebbinsPersonal Stebbins variant starting at a different value offset. Retains the formula benefits with a slightly less predictable opening sequence.
My CHaSeDPersonal CHaSeD stack grouped in sets of 4 by suit. Returns to its original order in exactly 2 Out-Faros — useful for resetting quickly in performance.
ParticleJoshua Jay's memorized stack. A Stay Stack with top/bottom mirror symmetry — the top 26 and bottom 26 mirror each other in value and color, enabling a unique set of visual and mathematical effects.
JoyalThe Joyal memorized stack. Known for strong poker hand demonstrations and a balanced suit distribution that supports both memdeck and gambling-style routines.
MATHEMATICAL STACKS
Stebbins +4Si Stebbins variant where each card’s value increases by 4 (instead of 3), suits cycling CHaSeD. Produces a denser mathematical pattern. Fully cyclical — position of any card is instantly calculable.
Stebbins +5Si Stebbins variant with value increment of 5. Suits cycle CHaSeD. Less predictable visually than +3 but retains the same instant position-calculation benefit.
Latin FormulaAncient cyclic stack (Rosary/Chapelet) based on a Latin mnemonic phrase: Unus quinque novem famulus sex quatuour duo Rex septem octo faemina trina decem — encoding the value sequence A,5,9,J,6,4,2,K,7,8,Q,3,10 cycling in CHaSeD suit order. One of the oldest published stack systems, first described by Guyot in 1769.

FARO NAVIGATOR & CYCLES
Faro NavigatorEnter a target position (2–52) to calculate the exact sequence of In and Out shuffles needed to move the current top card there. Based on the binary method by S. Brent Morris: subtract 1 from the target, convert to binary, then read each bit as an In-shuffle (1) or Out-shuffle (0). Use Step or Run All to execute the sequence live on the deck.
8 Out-FarosOne of the most remarkable results in card mathematics: 8 consecutive Out-Faros return any 52-card deck to its exact original order. Step through one shuffle at a time or Run All — the progress bar fills and a confirmation appears when every card is back in place.
52 In-FarosThe In-Faro equivalent: 52 consecutive In-Faros restore the deck — one shuffle for every card. The cycle length matches the deck size exactly, which feels almost impossibly poetic. Use Reset at any point to return the deck to its state when the demo started.
