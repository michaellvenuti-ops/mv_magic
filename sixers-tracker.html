How it works (Mack, Vol. 2 — player-based prop models, in Python). For each Sixers starter and each of points / rebounds / assists: (1) Base = the player's last-10-games per-minute rate x his last-10 average minutes. (2) Opponent adjustment — points scale by opponent defensive rating vs the league average; every stat scales by opponent pace vs league average; points get a +1% home-court bump. Opponent ratings come from the NBA Overs scan's daily team-stats snapshot. (3) Sigma = the standard deviation of the model's own residuals — for every game the player has played this season, the model projects that game from its minutes/opponent/home and the spread of actual-minus-projection is the sigma (floored: PTS 4.0, REB 2.0, AST 1.5). (4) Probability = Normal distribution, P(Over) = 1 − Φ((line − projection) / sigma). A prop flags when the model's edge over the book's implied probability is ≥ 5%, and the flagged side also hit in at least 4 of the player's last 10 games. Paper only — $100 flat per flag at the posted price, graded the next morning. Lines: HaterPicks public game page (best Over/Under price at tracked US books, recorded at scan time). A starter listed OUT is skipped; a questionable starter is flagged with a QUESTIONABLE tag and a DNP grades as a void. Note: an early per-player regression on 13–17 games was tried and dropped — it projected Embiid at 15.8 points vs a 20.0 last-10 mean. The factor model above is the flagger.
