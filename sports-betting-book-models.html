📘 Miller & Davidow — The Logic of Sports Betting

(17 May 2019, ~230 pp., ISBN 978-1-0968-0572-4) — Three parts: Bets and Books, The Logic, Winning. Important caveat from the authors themselves: it contains no ready-made predictive model and no Kelly chapter — modelling is the one key area they say they do not cover. If we paper-trade three next, start here

Chosen for buildability + fit with what you already run. Your Sixers Tracker already covers the spirit of Mack 2.2 / 2.4 (BPM + player-prop simulation), so these three add something new instead of duplicating it.

01 — Easiest win

Elo team ratings (Mack 2.1)

Results only, one K-factor per sport. Gives you a clean moneyline / spread probability for any league — even low-data ones — to compare against the book every morning.

02 — Fits your arb scans

Synthetic hold — “chopping the hold” (Logic L2)

Build a near-0% market from the best price on each side across books. Only bet when synthetic hold is ~0% or negative — then a tiny real edge is enough. Direct extension of your arbitrage work.

03 — Validates everything

Closing-line value tracker (Logic L3 + L15)

Log price taken vs. closing price for every paper bet. Average CLV of at least half the hold over hundreds of bets is the book’s “promising” test. Without this, win % alone tells you almost nothing.

Mack Vol. 1 — Team-level, generalised models

The toolkit book: ratings, regression, simulation, backtesting. Mack’s own warning runs through it — a model is only a tool to find an effect; the effect, not the model, is the edge. Beware the “generalised model” trap of one model for all sports.

6 models
Mack 1.1 • Vol. 1Easy

Bradley–Terry team-rating model

The author’s own example of a Vol. 1 model. Each team gets a latent strength; win probability comes from the strength ratio.

Sport
Any team sport — author recommends low-limit, data-poor leagues, e.g. Icelandic women’s basketball
Predicts
Win probability for a match-up
Inputs
Historical head-to-head / win–loss results only, plus home advantage
Math
P(i beats j) = strengthi / (strengthi + strengthj), fitted by maximum likelihood / iterative solving in Excel
Bet
Moneyline (spread/total derived from it)
Watch out — Author warns it loses badly in major leagues (NBA) where player data matters. Data needed: results only.

Mack 1.5 • Vol. 1Medium

Benter boost — blend model + market probability

A probability-combination layer, not a bet type. Named for horse-racing originator Bill Benter.

Sport
Any sport
Predicts
Improved win probability
Inputs
Your model’s probability + the market’s implied probability (from odds)
Math
Second-stage combination (in Benter’s original work, a logit combining model & public probabilities); use combined figure for EV/Kelly decisions, and as a test of whether your model adds anything
Bet
Any — it improves the probability you feed into every other model
Not verified — The blend concept is verified as Benter’s method in wider literature; Mack’s exact Excel implementation (weight/formula) was not verified. Hard to calibrate without out-of-sample data.
Mack 1.6 • Vol. 1Easy to run

Backtesting + walk-forward validation

A method, not a bet — the layer that decides whether any model above deserves paper money, let alone real money.

Sport
All
Predicts
Whether a model/angle actually beats the vig
Inputs
Historical results + historical (or closing) odds
Math
Backtest ROI vs. closing line; Mack prefers walk-forward (fit, then test on new in-season data as it arrives) and a Bayesian-updated Kelly test — start with a Kelly stake prior, shrink it as results arrive, discard the model when it hits zero
Bet
Validation layer for all models
Watch out — Backtests assume a static market; Mack warns markets adapt and edges erode. Segment tests by month / season phase (e.g. NHL pre- vs. post-trade-deadline).

The Logic of Sports Betting — Market rules you can systematise

No predictive model in here — and the authors are explicit about that. Instead: how to price, where books are weak, and how to know if you’re actually winning. Market-maker books post openers and move on action; most retail books copy them. That gap is where L3 / L4 / L12 live — and why they decay as books improve.

15 frameworks
Logic L1Easy

Break-even % and EV

Convert every price to the win % needed to profit; judge bets by % edge, not points.

Needs
Odds from any book
Rule
EV = (your true probability × decimal odds) − 1; bet only if positive. −110 ⇒ 52.4% break-even. A 12/1 shot moving to 7/1 is a 4% edge; an even-money shot moving to 8/11 is an 8% edge — the latter is better despite fewer “points” gained.
Bet
All markets
Hard part — The spreadsheet is easy; the hard input is your true probability.
Logic L2Easy–Medium

Synthetic hold / “chopping the hold” to ~0%

Build a market from the best price on each side across books — and across related markets.

Needs
Live odds from many books (market-maker + retail), plus push-rate charts to convert between spreads/totals/derivatives
Rule
Synthetic hold = sum of implied probabilities of best prices − 1. Book example: Dolphins/Bills −5.5 at −110/−110 (4.5% hold) vs. best prices +100/−110 (2.3% hold). Paper rule: only bet when synthetic hold is near 0% (negative = arbitrage). Extend shopping to related markets (−6.5 vs −6 vs moneyline) and derivatives to chop further.
Bet
Spread / total / moneyline + derivatives
⭐ Top candidate — Odds comparison is easy; push-rate charts need 10 years of historical final scores by league.
Logic L3Easy

Closing-line value (CLV) + market agreement / resistance

The book’s validation metric — more trustworthy than short-run win %.

Needs
Your bet price + closing price at market-maker books, over hundreds of bets
Rule
Move in your favour = agreement (good); move against you = resistance (red flag — stop, diagnose, do not double down / top up, or your biggest positions land on your worst bets). Average CLV of at least half the hold over hundreds of bets is promising (e.g. bet at 42% break-even, closes at 45% in a 4%-hold market = 3% CLV).
Bet
Major markets (validation); log CLV per paper bet
Scope — Applies only in liquid, true markets (NFL spread, MLB moneyline, NBA total) — not in un-bet retail props.
Logic L4Easy as filter

Strong vs. weak markets + attack surface

Bet where books are weakest, not where they are sharpest.

Needs
Knowledge of which books are market makers vs. retail copiers; line-movement data
Rule
Strong: full-game spread/moneyline/total and (NFL/NBA) first halves — price-discovered, heavily picked-over, avoid. Weak: derivatives, props, multiway markets — books expand the menu faster than they can price it; openers are guessed or copied from stale charts and often never updated. Allocate paper trading to weak markets only.
Bet
Derivatives, props, multiways
Build note — Easy as a filter; Medium to execute (requires watching specific books’ menus daily).
Logic L5Medium

Pricing related markets (push-rate charts)

Derive a fair price for one market from a strong market.

Needs
10 years of historical game results (final scores/margins) by league
Rule
Build a chart of how often games land on each margin (NFL key numbers: 3, 7, etc.). From a strong spread/total, estimate the five probabilities needed to price another spread (e.g. −28: dog wins, tie, fav wins by <28, exactly 28, >28) and hence its fair price. Same method prices alt spreads/totals and full-game → 1H/1Q conversions.
Bet
Alt spreads/totals, 1H/1Q/1P derivatives
Data — Historical scores are public (e.g. Pro-Football-Reference); the chart is a one-off build.
Logic L6Medium

Derivatives — 6-step process

A checklist for hunting mispriced segment markets, no model required for the qualitative version.

Needs
Derivative openers + posting times per book, market spread/total, hold, news (lineups, weather, opener pitchers, platoon splits)
Rule
1) Find a derivative at a retail book. 2) Watch it: posting time, opener system (ideally a simple function of game total). 3) Judge pick-through (fewer books / no movement = better). 4) Check hold (4% workable; 6%+ needs real math). 5) Find outlier situations where the book’s method fails — worked example: MLB 1st-inning “score?” where opener pitcher is weak, Yankees stack lefties, best hitters now bat 1-2-3-4, chart is 6 years old. 6) Track & refine. Example price: Yes +140 / No −160 (≈3% hold).
Bet
Derivatives (1Q/1P/1st-inning, alt lines)
Logic L7Medium

Props — 5-step process

Player props + game props. Core insight: props are priced off the main line at posting and not updated.

Needs
Prop openers vs. main-market moves, injury/lineup/weather news, spread/total per game
Rule
1) Find a prop whose fair price should vary a lot with spread/total. 2) Reverse-engineer its pricing over many games. 3) Compare across books. 4) Watch whether others bet it / how the book moves it. 5) Bet when price ignores spread/total. Examples: pitcher K under still available after two high-K hitters are benched; receiver yards prop unchanged after his shadowing CB is ruled out. Game-prop fair values from author’s NFL model: “score three consecutive times” 61% (pick’em) to 76% (17-pt favourite, low total) vs 4–5% hold; “odd/even final score” only 52–54% — too small to beat hold.
Bet
Player props, game/team props
Bottleneck — News monitoring. The game-prop version is fully systematizable with a spread/total → fair-price table.
Logic L8Medium

Angles — 3 tests

Before any angle is bettable, it must pass all three.

Needs
Historical data grouped by closing total/spread; recent vs. old seasons
Rule
(a) Predictable (applies to most games), (b) Quantifiable (measure its size in %), (c) Not accounted for in the line — check by reverse-engineering how the book builds its opener (usually a backward-looking league-average chart). Worked example: NFL 1st-quarter unders — 2nd-half scoring efficiency has risen since ~2017, so a given total now means less 1Q scoring than a 2005–2014 chart implies. Bet the angle in obscure derivatives, not 1H markets where discovery has absorbed it.
Bet
Derivatives (esp. 1Q/1P totals)
Dated example — The specific 1Q-under angle is from 2019 — re-quantify, do not blindly bet it.
Logic L9Easy

Parlays — uses for winners

Two uses verified in detail; a third is in the chapter but was not verified — see Open Questions.

Needs
Your +EV straight bets; book limits per market
Rule
(i) Compound edge: at 55% at −110, straight ROI 5%, 2-leg parlay ROI 10.1% (but −EV compounds identically: 50% ⇒ −4.6% straight, −9% parlayed). (ii) Circumvent limits: round-robin-by-3s turns a $50/game limit into ~$600 volume (book’s Korean-baseball example: 4 games × $50 straights = $200; four 3-leg $50 parlays ≈ $600).
Bet
Parlays / round robins
Build note — Pure staking arithmetic once you have +EV legs; needs books that don’t slash parlay limits.
Logic L10Medium–Hard

Multiway markets & futures

Much larger holds, harder to price — opportunity and trap in equal measure.

Needs
All prices in the multiway/futures market; equivalent rolling moneyline parlay prices
Rule
Only bet when you can show the futures price is better than the equivalent series of moneyline bets rolled forward, or when public foolishness creates a clear misprice.
Bet
Futures, multiways
Not verified in detail — Chapter-level rule only; specifics were not verified. Requires a full-field probability model.
Logic L11Hard to automate

In-play / live betting

Vendor-feed errors, timeouts-only, and the halftime discontinuity.

Needs
Live odds feed, game state, pace/foul/lineup observations; TV-delay awareness
Rule
(a) Bet only during timeouts — TV is ~18s delayed and books add 4–8s bet delay, so live betting is betting a stale price into ~6.5% hold. (b) Bet the vendor model’s mistakes: in-play prices use league-average assumptions — e.g. NHL 2nd-period scoring is higher (long change), goalie-pull habits vary by team, NBA 4th-quarter pace skews when lopsided, key players in early foul trouble in CBB. (c) Halftime discontinuity: halftime lines are price-discovered, then the in-play feed restarts ignoring that action — if the in-play total pops back near the halftime opener, that is a free under (per the book). (d) Don’t “wait for a better price” on a favourite in-game.
Bet
Live spread/total/derivatives
Build note — Medium as a manual checklist; Hard to automate. Timeout/halftime rules are directly paper-tradable.
Logic L12Easy to spot

Steam chasing (part of “chopping the hold”)

Use a sharp move as evidence for your derivative bet — when the slow book hasn’t moved yet.

Needs
Market-maker line moves in real time; retail derivative prices
Rule
When a market-maker total/spread moves (“steam”) but a retail book hasn’t moved its related derivative, count it as ~0.5% of hold-chopping in the book’s worked example.
Bet
Derivatives at slow retail books
Secondary claim — Secondary sources quote the book as warning steam chasing is also the fastest way to get limited/banned — treat as secondary, not verified in the text read. Hard in practice (speed + account longevity).

Logic L14Easy

“60% or 54%?” — volume over win rate

A mindset/staking rule, not a model.

Needs
Your edge estimates across candidate bets
Rule
Bet every bet you believe is +EV, even small-edge ones; don’t chase a high win % by passing 55% bets while waiting for 67% ones — the many small-edge bets earn more in total. Paper rule: log every +EV candidate and bet (on paper) all of them; judge by total profit and CLV, not win %.
Bet
All
Logic L15Easy

How do I know if I’m winning? — validation

The closing discipline that ties back to CLV (L3).

Needs
Full bet record (price taken, stake, result, closing price)
Rule
Keep records; after ~1,000 settled bets the record starts to speak; but the decisive test is CLV plus being able to name why you are right and the book is wrong (more information, or better use of available information). Paper rule: paper-trade at least hundreds of bets and require average CLV > half the hold before staking real money.

⚠ Open questions — what the research could not verify
