1. The derived rule (plainly)

Todd bets in-game pace — but he does not follow it blindly. He matches the pace projection about 3 times in 4, and he overrides it on game situation, and the overrides are where his edge over a pure pace rule lives.

Stated as a rule:

  1. Wait for pace to establish. He normally does NOT bet at the first (under-16-min) timeout. He waits until the pace is visible, then bets the side the pace points to. (His own words: the Siena Under was “a classic mistake” for betting too early; he “almost def would have been on 1h over at some point later in the half.”)
  2. 1st-half totals: bet the OVER when the early pace is hot. He is heavily over-leaning on 1H (41 Overs vs 16 Unders across all 57 picks; 30 of 57 picks are 1H). He will take a 1H over on a thin projected edge — the St Mary’s 1H over was taken on a projection only ~2 points above the line.
  3. Full-game totals at halftime: bet the side the 1st-half pace implies (projection = H1 x 2). All three full-game-at-halftime Unders matched the pace projection. But he overrides to the OVER when a big favorite trails at half (Boise State, a 12.5-pt favorite trailing at half, H1 63 → proj 126 vs line 151.5 — he bet OVER anyway and won; the 2H scored 89).
  4. Matchup override on 1H overs. Miami 1H over 71.5: the early pace pointed UNDER (10-8 at ~6 min elapsed, proj ~60), but he bet OVER on matchup (Miami a big favorite vs overmatched Manhattan) and won by 11.5 (H1 83).
  5. FT shooting + bonus is a secondary input. He watches FT-shooting rank and bonus status (Wright State: “30th in the nation in FT shooting and in the bonus for the last 12 mins of this 1h”). Missed FTs / missed one-and-ones repeatedly kill his overs (Idaho State, Delaware, Miami, IU Indy — IU Indy finished 19-of-30 FT).

Threshold: No clear minimum margin is visible. He took a 1H over on a ~2-point projected edge (St Mary’s) and full-game-at-halftime unders on 8.5–18.5-point projected edges. If a threshold exists, it is small (≤ ~2 points on 1H overs). Sample too small to pin down.

1H vs full-game: The rule differs by period. 1H picks are overwhelmingly Overs and are the money-loser (see §3). Full-game picks (pregame + at-halftime) are more balanced and profitable. The full-game-at-halftime unders are the most pace-faithful subset (3 of 3 matched pace; 2 of 3 won).

6. Confidence and limits

  • Sample size is the binding constraint. The pace hypothesis is tested on 8 picks with a real post-time state. A 6-of-8 match rate (75%) is suggestive, not proof — the 95% confidence interval on 6/8 is roughly 35%–97%. The Todd-vs-rule dollar gap (+$134 vs -$227) is driven by 2 picks (Miami, Boise).
  • The grading is partial. Only 35 of 56 CBB total picks (62.5%) could be graded at $100 flat. The 20 UNKNOWN picks are concentrated on Nov 20–22 (when fewer finals were pulled). If the UNKNOWN picks skew one way, Todd’s overall +$238.90 could move materially.
  • Post-time state is reconstructed, not observed. No public source gives wall-clock-tagged plays. The 1H anchors rely on Todd’s own clock statements (Idaho State’s two anchors are mutually inconsistent as literal clock readings — “needed 25 in last 8 mins” at 4:41 PM vs “need 19 last 4 mins” at 4:42 PM — so they are treated as approximate) and on a recap’s clock mention (Miami). The full-game-at-halftime projections are exact (H1 x 2) and are the strongest evidence.
  • The “pure pace rule” is a strawman in one respect: it bets the projection side at -110 with no threshold and no situational override. A real pace model would use a threshold and would account for the trailing-favorite / matchup effects Todd uses. Todd beating this naive rule does not prove pace is useless — it proves Todd adds value beyond naive pace in this small sample.
  • What the data CAN prove: Todd does read in-game pace (6/8 match, and his own words confirm the process); his 1H overs are the money-loser in this window; his full-game and at-halftime picks are profitable in this window; late-half droughts and missed FTs are the recurring way his 1H overs die.
  • What it CANNOT prove: a precise threshold; whether the pace edge is real or noise; whether the situational overrides (trailing favorite, matchup) are systematically profitable rather than two lucky wins; anything about his long-run edge (his own season tally, 166-134 +14.27u, is a separate, larger sample that this 3-day window cannot validate).
